Hypertension: Diagnosis and Treatment

Hypertension (HTN) affects more than 1.28 billion adults worldwide and is the leading modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease (CVD), stroke, and chronic kidney disease. Despite effective treatments, only approximately 20% of hypertensive patients achieve adequate blood pressure (BP) control globally.

Definition and Classification

Blood pressure classification (ACC/AHA 2017 guidelines):

  • Normal: systolic <120 mmHg and diastolic <80 mmHg
  • Elevated: systolic 120-129 mmHg and diastolic <80 mmHg
  • Stage 1 HTN: systolic 130-139 mmHg or diastolic 80-89 mmHg
  • Stage 2 HTN: systolic ≥140 mmHg or diastolic ≥90 mmHg
  • Hypertensive crisis: systolic >180 mmHg and/or diastolic >120 mmHg

ESC/ESH 2018 guidelines define hypertension as office BP ≥140/90 mmHg.

Diagnosis and Measurement

Accurate BP measurement is essential. Guidelines recommend:

  • Patient seated and rested for 5 minutes
  • Average of at least 2 readings taken 1-2 minutes apart
  • Calibrated, validated sphygmomanometer with appropriate cuff size
  • Confirm diagnosis with out-of-office measurements (ABPM or HBPM)

Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) is the gold standard, capturing daytime average, nocturnal dipping, and BP variability. White-coat hypertension (office BP elevated, ABPM normal) occurs in 15-30% of patients. Masked hypertension (normal office BP, elevated ABPM) carries the same cardiovascular risk as sustained hypertension.

Secondary Hypertension

Secondary causes should be considered in patients with resistant hypertension, age <30 years, or sudden onset. Common causes:

  • Renovascular hypertension (renal artery stenosis)
  • Primary aldosteronism (Conn syndrome): most common secondary cause (~10%)
  • Phaeochromocytoma / paraganglioma
  • Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)
  • Cushing syndrome
  • Hypothyroidism / hyperthyroidism
  • Coarctation of the aorta (young patients)

Cardiovascular Risk Assessment

Total cardiovascular risk should guide treatment intensity. Risk calculators include SCORE2 (European) and ASCVD Pooled Cohort Equations (US). High-risk conditions warranting immediate treatment regardless of BP stage:

  • Established CVD (coronary artery disease, stroke, PAD)
  • Diabetes mellitus
  • Chronic kidney disease (CKD) stage ≥3
  • LVEF <40%

Treatment

Lifestyle Modification

Non-pharmacological interventions are recommended for all patients:

  • DASH diet: reduces systolic BP by 8-14 mmHg
  • Sodium restriction (<2.3 g/day): reduces systolic BP by 5-6 mmHg
  • Weight loss: 1 mmHg reduction per 1 kg weight loss
  • Aerobic exercise (≥150 min/week moderate intensity)
  • Alcohol limitation (≤14 units/week)
  • Smoking cessation (does not lower BP but reduces overall CVD risk)

Pharmacological Treatment

First-line antihypertensive agents (all reduce major cardiovascular events):

  • Thiazide/thiazide-like diuretics: chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg/day, indapamide 1.5-2.5 mg/day
  • ACE inhibitors: ramipril 2.5-10 mg/day, perindopril 4-8 mg/day, enalapril 5-40 mg/day
  • Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs): losartan 25-100 mg/day, olmesartan 10-40 mg/day
  • Dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (CCBs): amlodipine 2.5-10 mg/day, felodipine 2.5-10 mg/day

Combination therapy is required in most patients to achieve BP targets. Single-pill combinations (SPCs) improve adherence and are preferred. Triple therapy (RAS blocker + CCB + diuretic) achieves control in most patients.

Resistant hypertension (BP uncontrolled on 3+ agents including a diuretic at optimal doses) should prompt spironolactone 25-50 mg/day as a fourth agent, or referral for specialist evaluation and investigation of secondary causes.

BP Targets

ACC/AHA 2017: <130/80 mmHg for most patients; <130/80 mmHg for patients with diabetes, CKD, CVD, or high CV risk.

ESC/ESH 2018: initial target <140/90 mmHg; if tolerated, consider <130/80 mmHg in most patients aged <65 years.

Hypertensive Emergency

Hypertensive emergency: severe BP elevation (>180/120 mmHg) with acute target organ damage (TOD). Examples include hypertensive encephalopathy, acute coronary syndrome, acute aortic dissection, acute HF with pulmonary oedema, and hypertensive nephrosclerosis.

Management: IV antihypertensives in ICU setting. Reduce mean arterial pressure (MAP) by no more than 25% in the first hour, then to 160/100-110 mmHg over 2-6 hours (except aortic dissection: target <120/80 mmHg urgently). Agents: IV labetalol, nicardipine, sodium nitroprusside, clevidipine.
