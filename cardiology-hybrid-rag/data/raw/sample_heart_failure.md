# Heart Failure: Classification, Diagnosis, and Management

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical syndrome characterised by the heart's
inability to pump sufficient blood to meet the body's metabolic demands, or
doing so only at the expense of elevated filling pressures. It affects
approximately 64 million people globally and carries a 5-year mortality
rate exceeding 50%.

## Classification by Ejection Fraction

The 2021 ESC Guidelines classify HF by left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF):

| Phenotype | LVEF        | Abbreviation |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| Reduced   | < 40%       | HFrEF        |
| Mildly reduced | 40-49% | HFmrEF      |
| Preserved | >= 50%      | HFpEF        |

HFrEF accounts for approximately 50% of all HF cases. HFpEF is increasingly
prevalent, particularly in older women with hypertension, obesity, and
diabetes.

## Aetiology

Common causes by phenotype:

HFrEF:
- Ischaemic cardiomyopathy (coronary artery disease): ~50% of cases
- Dilated cardiomyopathy (idiopathic, genetic, toxic: alcohol, chemotherapy)
- Myocarditis (viral, autoimmune)
- Valvular heart disease (aortic regurgitation, mitral regurgitation)

HFpEF:
- Hypertension (most common)
- Obesity and metabolic syndrome
- Atrial fibrillation
- Infiltrative disease (amyloidosis, haemochromatosis)

## Diagnosis

### Symptoms and Signs

Symptoms: dyspnoea (exertional, orthopnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea),
fatigue, ankle oedema, reduced exercise tolerance.

Signs: elevated JVP, S3 gallop, bilateral basal crackles, peripheral oedema,
ascites, hepatomegaly.

### Investigations

- ECG: may show LVH, bundle branch block, AF, Q waves (prior MI)
- Chest X-ray: cardiomegaly, pulmonary oedema (Kerley B lines, bat-wing pattern)
- Echocardiography: essential — LVEF, wall motion, valvular pathology, diastolic function
- BNP/NT-proBNP: BNP > 35 pg/mL or NT-proBNP > 125 pg/mL supports HF diagnosis
- Cardiac MRI: gold standard for myocardial characterisation

## Treatment

### Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT) for HFrEF

The four pillars of GDMT reduce mortality and hospitalisation:

1. ACE inhibitors or ARBs (if ACE-I intolerant):
   - Enalapril 2.5-20 mg BID, ramipril 2.5-10 mg/day
   - Losartan 25-150 mg/day, candesartan 4-32 mg/day

2. Beta-blockers (only after clinical stability):
   - Carvedilol 3.125-25 mg BID
   - Metoprolol succinate 12.5-200 mg/day
   - Bisoprolol 1.25-10 mg/day

3. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs):
   - Spironolactone 25-50 mg/day
   - Eplerenone 25-50 mg/day
   - Monitor K+ and renal function closely (risk of hyperkalaemia)

4. SGLT2 inhibitors (mortality benefit independent of diabetes):
   - Dapagliflozin 10 mg/day
   - Empagliflozin 10 mg/day

ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan 24/26 - 97/103 mg BID) replaces ACE-I/ARB in
selected patients who remain symptomatic on optimised GDMT.

### Device Therapy

- ICD: primary prevention in HFrEF with LVEF <= 35% on optimal GDMT
  (at least 3 months after initiation) in patients with life expectancy > 1 year
- CRT-D: LBBB with QRS >= 150 ms and LVEF <= 35% and NYHA class II-III

### Decongestive Therapy

Loop diuretics (furosemide 20-500 mg/day) remain the cornerstone of
symptom management and are titrated to maintain euvolaemia.

## Monitoring and Follow-Up

- BNP/NT-proBNP guided therapy may help optimise GDMT
- Renal function, electrolytes, and BP every 1-2 weeks during up-titration
- 6-minute walk test and NYHA class reassessment at each visit
- Remote haemodynamic monitoring (CardioMEMS) reduces HF hospitalisation
  in NYHA Class III patients

## Prognosis

One-year mortality is approximately 20-30% for hospitalised HF patients.
The MAGGIC meta-analysis identified predictors of poor prognosis:
male sex, lower LVEF, lower systolic BP, higher NYHA class, lower BMI,
current smoking, diabetes, and renal impairment.
